How to read a TMA output

Every figure is labelled by how it was produced

So that a directly measured grain property is never confused with a model prediction or a commercial estimate.

Measured
Directly measured

A physical property read from the grain image — length, width, aspect ratio, projected chalky area, grain count.

AI-Derived
Model classification

A class assigned by a trained model — head rice, broken, chalky, discoloured, immature, paddy — and the indices built from those classes.

Predicted
Predictive output

A modelled estimate of a property not directly visible. Indicative — not a substitute for certified laboratory testing.

Calculated
Commercial calculation

An economic figure computed from quality data together with prices and quantities entered by the mill.

Inside the Analytics view

Built for sample-to-sample detail

Every chart is driven by individual samples, not just daily averages — with a dedicated chart per parameter so different units and scales never get muddled together.

Sample-to-Sample Trend Charts

Every sample plotted in order, labelled by sample number and date, one chart per parameter.

Control Charts

Mean and upper/lower control limits overlaid on the trend, so drift and instability are visible immediately.

Best, Worst & Abnormal Samples

The strongest sample, the weakest sample, and any statistical outliers are surfaced automatically.

Machine-to-Machine Comparison

Grouped comparisons across every machine, plus a machine × day quality heatmap.

Series-to-Series Comparison

Compare full multi-machine production runs against one another, not just single machines.

Variety Breakdowns

Composition and radar views showing how quality differs from one paddy or rice variety to the next.

Predictive analytics

Go beyond appearance. Understand the grain.

Some of the things a buyer cares about most — how the rice cooks, how it eats, what it carries nutritionally — cannot be seen on the surface of a grain. TMA models them from the imaging data it already collects, and reports them as clearly labelled predictions alongside the measured results.

Important. The outputs below are AI-based predictive estimates. They are not certified laboratory chemical analysis and are not a substitute for accredited testing, unless APIT has published validation supporting that specific use.

Setting the Polisher

Degree of Milling and Degree of Nutrition move in opposite directions. Seeing both lets a mill choose where on that curve it wants to sell.

Matching a Buyer

Cooking behaviour is what a food-service or retail buyer actually specifies. Predicted cooking indicators help match a lot to the customer it suits.

Fortification Programmes

Indicative micronutrient outputs support internal screening and prioritisation of samples for confirmatory laboratory testing.

Predictive modules are offered as decision-support tools for internal process and commercial judgement. APIT does not present nutritional or micronutrient predictions as certified laboratory chemical analysis. Availability of individual predictive parameters is configuration-dependent and subject to model validation for the variety and process concerned.
